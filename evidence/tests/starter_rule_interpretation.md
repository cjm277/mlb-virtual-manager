## Interpretation

**The rule as specified (at most 2 extra starts) is worth +0.08 verified wins per 162 (−0.02 to +0.18).** It is now the Pitching Coach's starting point in the app (the `model_starter` tool). Against a plain rotation, which comes out at −0.07 when replayed this way, it gains about 0.15 wins. Rest-of-season results agree it doesn't lose anything (−0.00).

**The cap is the whole trade-off.** Every extra start the cap allows moves value up, in a straight line:

| Extra starts allowed | 0 | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 6 | none |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Verified wins | −0.06 | −0.01 | +0.08 | +0.16 | +0.24 | +0.32 | +0.37 |

The value comes from the best starters taking turns the fifth starter would have had, on 4 days of rest instead of 5. 2026 data shows no measurable performance penalty for 4 days of rest (Check 3 of the value verification). What the data can't price is the season-long workload: innings limits, and injury risk for a starter making 34-36 starts instead of 32. That is a club decision with medical input, which is why the cap exists. Real 2026 starters started on exactly 4 days of rest in 30% of starts; the rule raises that to 44% at the 2-start cap and 52% at 4.

**How this compares with the earlier starter piece (+0.22).** That figure came from the Pitching Coach's own one-game choices on 150 plans, scaled down by the average share that survives a season replay. The replay here measures a season policy directly, so it is the better number for the rule the app now follows. The total verified value is therefore:
- **+1.71 + 0.08 ≈ +1.8 wins** at the 2-start cap;
- **≈ +1.95** at a 4-start cap.

**Same yardstick as the lineup.** The rule ranks starters by the Marcel projection, so it is valued on Marcel times Marcel's own within-team slope against real runs (0.65; August-September hold-out 0.67). That is how the lineup's offense is valued. The older season-stats lens gives smaller numbers (+0.04 at the 2-start cap) because it disagrees with the projection the rule uses.
